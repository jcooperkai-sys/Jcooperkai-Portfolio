DESIGNING RELIABLE AGENTIC AI WORKFLOWS

Author: Cooper E.
Venue: Independent Research Note
Year: 2026

ABSTRACT

This ongoing research note studies how AI agents can plan, use tools, recover
from errors, and explain what they are doing without becoming brittle. It
outlines a practical architecture built around explicit state, small
verifiable steps, bounded tool access, and human-readable traces. The goal is
to turn capable prototypes into systems that remain understandable when a
task becomes long, ambiguous, or surprising.

1. MOTIVATION

Many agent demos optimize for finishing a task once. Reliable systems also
need to explain their current state, stop safely, and recover when a tool or
assumption fails. Reliability is therefore treated here as an interaction
design problem as much as a model-quality problem.

2. WORKFLOW DESIGN

The proposed workflow separates intent, plan, action, observation, and
verification. Each step records what changed and what evidence supports the
next step. Tools receive only the authority required for the current action,
and consequential operations are designed to be reversible or explicitly
confirmed.

3. EVALUATION PLAN

Experiments will compare completion quality, error recovery, trace clarity,
and unnecessary tool calls across increasingly long tasks. The current
document describes a research direction and evaluation plan; it does not
claim a finished benchmark result.

4. CONCLUSION

The core hypothesis is simple: agents become more useful when their work is
broken into inspectable state transitions and when failure is treated as a
normal event to recover from, not something to hide.
