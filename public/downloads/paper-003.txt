HUMAN-CENTERED CONTROLS FOR AUTONOMOUS SYSTEMS

Author: Cooper E.
Venue: Technical Memo
Year: 2026

ABSTRACT

This technical memo examines controls that help people supervise agentic
systems without needing to inspect every internal step. It focuses on
permission boundaries, reversible actions, visible checkpoints, and useful
failure messages. The central question is how to preserve creative autonomy
while keeping consequential actions legible and easy to stop.

1. CONTROL WITHOUT MICROMANAGEMENT

A useful control surface should expose decisions that change risk, authority,
or external state. It should not force a person to approve every harmless
intermediate calculation.

2. PRACTICAL PATTERNS

The memo develops four patterns: scoped permission envelopes, action previews,
recovery checkpoints, and compact event logs. Each pattern aims to make the
system easier to interrupt, audit, and resume.

3. OPEN QUESTIONS

Future experiments will study when checkpoints help or distract, which
explanations make failures actionable, and how interfaces should communicate
uncertainty without becoming noisy.

4. STATUS

This is an independent technical memo and active research direction. It is
shared to invite careful discussion, not to present a completed empirical
result.
